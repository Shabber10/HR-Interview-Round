### Q01. What would you do if assigned a task with zero documentation?
- **Answer**:
  > "I wouldn't wait around. First, I would inspect related code modules, check existing API endpoints, and research how similar features work. 
  > 
  > Then, I'd draft a quick 3-bullet plan of my proposed approach and verify it with my lead: *'Here is how I plan to build this—does this align with your expectations?'* Once confirmed, I document the steps so the next engineer has clear instructions."

---

### Q02. What would you do if asked to do something unethical or unsafe?
- **Answer**:
  > "I would stay calm and politely explain the exact security or data risk to the person asking. If they still insisted on bypassing safety or ethics policies, I would escalate through proper compliance or grievance channels. Protecting user data and company trust is non-negotiable."

---

### Q03. How would you handle a manager with an impatient or harsh communication style?
- **Answer**:
  > "I follow **MS Dhoni's rule: never take the tone personally**. If a manager is stressed, it means there is heavy deadline pressure. 
  > 
  > I make my updates short, fact-based, and focused on solutions: what is done, what is blocked, and how I am fixing it. Staying calm and clear resolves communication issues quickly."

---

### Q04. If you have two competing job offers, how will you decide which one to choose?
- **Answer**:
  > "I evaluate three things:
  > 1. **Quality of Mentorship & Learning**: Where will I build deeper, real-world engineering skills?
  > 2. **Team Culture**: Where is there a transparent, collaborative environment?
  > 3. **Product Vision**: Where is the company building products with a strong future?
  > As an early-career engineer, learning and great mentorship outweigh small salary differences."

---

### Q05. What if you work hard on a feature for two weeks and it gets completely canceled?
- **Answer**:
  > "I understand that business priorities shift based on market feedback. The effort isn't wasted—I learned new technical skills while writing that code, and I archive it cleanly in Git in case we need it later. Then I immediately redirect 100% of my energy to the new priority."

---

### Q06. What would you do if a user discovered a security bug or vulnerability in your code?
- **Answer**:
  > "Because cybersecurity is one of my passions, I would never get defensive. I would acknowledge the issue, reproduce the vulnerability in a local sandbox, deploy a patch immediately, and add an automated test to ensure that vulnerability can never reoccur."

---

### Q07. How do you handle working on weekends during emergency production releases?
- **Answer**:
  > "My NCC background trained me to step up whenever the team needs me. While regular planning should keep work sustainable, I am 100% ready to put in the hours during critical release emergencies to support my team."

---

### Q08. What would you do if you realized you gave an incomplete answer in this interview?
- **Answer**:
  > "I would address it directly: *'Excuse me, I realized that my earlier answer about [Topic] was incomplete. What I wanted to emphasize was [Point]. I wanted to clarify that.'* Honesty and presence of mind are always better than letting an error slide."

---

### Q09. If you see a colleague violating company data security policies, what do you do?
- **Answer**:
  > "Data security is everyone's responsibility. If it is a serious violation or data leak, I would report it immediately to the security team or HR through official confidential channels to protect company and customer data."

---

### Q10. How would you handle a situation where credit for your work was claimed by someone else?
- **Answer**:
  > "I wouldn't create drama. In our next 1-on-1, I would politely share my commit history and contribution notes with my lead. Going forward, I make sure to post regular progress updates in shared team channels so contributions are visible naturally."

---

### Q11. What would you do if assigned to a legacy tech stack you didn't prefer?
- **Answer**:
  > "Every tech stack teaches you something valuable about architecture, reliability, or legacy design. I would give my 100% to master it and deliver great work, while continuing to practice AI and modern stacks in my own time."

---

### Q12. How do you handle collaborating with teammates across different time zones?
- **Answer**:
  > "Clear written documentation is key. At the end of my day, I leave clear notes on what was completed, what is pending, and where the code branch is located so teammates in other time zones can pick it up immediately."

---

### Q13. If another company offers you a higher salary next month, will you leave us?
- **Answer**:
  > "No. I am looking for stability, skill-building, and a strong track record. Constantly switching for small increments hurts long-term career growth. I chose your company for its culture and learning, and I want to spend substantial time growing here."

---

### Q14. What would you do if given an impossible deadline?
- **Answer**:
  > "I wouldn't stay silent and fail at the end. I would break down the project into exact hours and present the trade-offs: *'To build this with complete testing takes 10 days. If we must ship in 5 days, which core feature should we prioritize for Phase 1?'* This turns a crisis into a practical business decision."

---

### Q15. How do you handle unexpected criticism in front of other team members?
- **Answer**:
  > "I stay composed, take notes without getting defensive, and focus on the technical substance of the feedback. After the meeting, I implement the fixes and follow up privately to confirm we are aligned."

---

### Q16. What would you do if asked to cut corners on security to ship faster?
- **Answer**:
  > "I would explain the risk: *'Skipping security checks might save a few hours today, but could lead to a data breach that costs the company months of reputation and money.'* I would propose automated security scans that run fast without delaying release."

---

### Q17. How do you evaluate if a workday was productive?
- **Answer**:
  > "A day was productive if I finished my top two priority tasks, unblocked a teammate if they needed help, and moved closer to our sprint goal without creating technical debt."

---

### Q18. What if you don't get along personally with someone on your project team?
- **Answer**:
  > "You don't need to be personal friends to be great teammates. I maintain high professionalism, communicate clearly and politely on all tasks, and keep our common project goals as the top priority."

---

### Q19. How do you handle tasks where you are blocked waiting for someone else?
- **Answer**:
  > "I send a polite reminder with the time I need their input by, and in the meantime, I work on unit tests, documentation, or pick up a pending backlog ticket so no time is wasted."

---

### Q20. What would you do if an error you made caused a production outage?
- **Answer**:
  > "I would take full ownership immediately. I would work with the team to restore service, diagnose the root cause, and implement an automated guardrail in CI/CD so that specific mistake can never happen again."

---

### Q21. Why do many freshers leave their first company quickly, and why won't you?
- **Answer**:
  > "Many people leave because of unrealistic expectations or chasing quick salary bumps without mastering core skills. I know the first two years are about building deep engineering discipline and a strong reputation. I am looking for long-term growth here."

---

### Q22. How do you handle conflicting instructions from two senior leads?
- **Answer**:
  > "I bring both viewpoints together politely: *'Senior A recommended Approach X, while Senior B suggested Approach Y. Let's do a quick 5-minute sync to align on the best path.'* This prevents confusion and keeps everyone on the same page."

---

### Q23. If you have an idea to improve a feature using AI automation, how would you pitch it?
- **Answer**:
  > "I would write a 1-page summary covering three points:
  > 1. What problem does it solve for users?
  > 2. How much time or cost will it save?
  > 3. A quick prototype showing it works.
  > Then I would ask for a 10-minute demo with my lead."

---

### Q24. What if after 6 months, you feel your learning curve is slowing down?
- **Answer**:
  > "First, I'd review my own output: Am I taking full ownership? Am I exploring edge cases? Then, I would speak with my lead in our 1-on-1: *'What advanced responsibilities or complex modules can I take on to deliver more value to the team?'*"
